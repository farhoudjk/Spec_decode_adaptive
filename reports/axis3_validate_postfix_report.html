Three earlier runs (original Axis-3, then validation rounds at rates 16/32 and 1/2) all showed the full arm's mean γ pinned at ~1.0–1.02 regardless of workload, rate, or the threshold recalibration meant to loosen it. The root cause, traced to raw step telemetry, was a wrong formula: tpot_ema was computing tokens produced per second across the whole batch, not seconds between one request's own tokens — a number that shrank as concurrency grew, in exactly the direction that kept GatedSpec's decode-bound gate closed. That formula is now fixed (tpot_ema tracks step_time_ema directly), and this run is the first real test of everything downstream of it.

The mechanism now works as designed

γ rises monotonically with load across all three rates, in the regime Axis-1 predicted γ could matter (decode-bound), and stays near the floor at rate=1 where the system is unconstrained and the gate should mostly stay shut. This is exactly the behavior the gate + acceptance-setpoint law were built to produce — it just needed a working input signal to act on.

Read

Working correctly is not the same as winning. At rate=4, full reaches γ≈2.2 on average versus static-cap-hi's fixed 4 — a lower average speculation depth, chosen because the acceptance-setpoint law (recalibrated threshold 0.15) computed that 2–3 is what the measured ~0.35–0.46 acceptance rate actually supports, not 4. SLO attainment lands within a percentage point either way (0.034 vs 0.038) — consistent with Axis-2's original finding that γ has limited leverage on outcomes in this regime, now measured with a controller that is actually reacting to real signal instead of silently doing nothing.
