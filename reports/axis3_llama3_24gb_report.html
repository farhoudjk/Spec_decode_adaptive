Swap the GPU, not the finding: the KV ceiling engages, but has no lever on committed decode

Same five-arm bracket, same reason workload, new pairing: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on a 24GB RTX A5000 (vs. Qwen2.5-7B on a 48GB A6000 for all four prior rounds). The smaller card + bigger target model finally pushes KV occupancy past the 0.80 ceiling trigger — something no A6000 run ever did, even at rate=32. KVAwareAdmit reacts hard, cutting the admission cap to its floor of 1. Mean KV occupancy barely moves anyway.

All four prior Axis-3 rounds ran Qwen2.5-7B on a 48GB A6000 and never got KV occupancy above 0.475 — nowhere near the 0.80 ceiling that KVAwareAdmit is built to react to. Open item #2 in that write-up was blunt: "The mechanism is untested end-to-end on hardware, not just unproven." Moving to a 24GB card with an 8B target model (nearly double the weight footprint, on half the memory) was the direct way to force that disturbance. It worked — static-cap-hi alone hits kv_frac=0.833 at rate=4 and 0.948 at rate=8. But the result is not the clean "ceiling engages, occupancy drops" story the design predicted.

Ceiling engaged, occupancy didn't follow

This is not the KV ceiling failing to trigger — it triggers hard and holds the cap at 1 for roughly 85% of the run. It's the ceiling triggering correctly and still not moving mean occupancy, because a lower admission cap only blocks new requests. It cannot evict or shrink the KV footprint of requests already running — and on reason (CNN-DailyMail, ~840 mean output tokens per request), once ~30 concurrent requests are mid-generation, their committed KV blocks are what's pinning occupancy near 1.0, not new arrivals.

What the ceiling actually did, one run

Step-level trace from kv-aware-cap-only_reason_r4.0_s0/steps.jsonl, the deepest KV-pressure cell where the ceiling is live. Cap collapses within ~500 steps of ramp-up; KV occupancy stays pinned regardless.

Does the ceiling change anything vs. no ceiling at all?

kv-aware-cap-only (TTFT-slack + KV ceiling, γ pinned) vs. adaptive-cap-only (TTFT-slack alone, γ pinned) — isolates the ceiling's incremental effect once the queue-slack loop is already accounted for.

Read

Mean KV occupancy is statistically indistinguishable between the two arms at every rate (max gap 0.05, within single-seed noise) — but e2e p95 is consistently worse with the ceiling active: +7.8s at rate=4, +9.8s at rate=8. The ceiling adds queueing delay (new requests wait longer to be admitted) without buying back KV headroom, because the headroom it's trying to protect is already spoken for by requests it can't touch. On this workload, KVAwareAdmit's core assumption — that gating admission prevents KV exhaustion — doesn't hold once decode length is long enough that steady-state occupancy is dominated by the running set, not the arrival rate.

Gated γ: shut for 85% of the run, by design

Step-level trace from full_reason_r4.0_s0. GatedSpec requires KV headroom (kv_headroom_frac: 0.15) before it opens. Since KV sits at ~0.97–1.0 for nearly the entire run, the gate stays shut — correctly, since spending more speculative compute per step while the KV pool is nearly full is exactly the wrong move. γ only climbs (to 3, then 6) in the last ~10% of the run as the queue drains.

This part is working as designed

Unlike the tpot_ema bug that pinned γ at the floor across four A6000 rounds, this is the gate doing its job: KV headroom is the correct reason to keep γ low here, and the controller is reading that signal accurately. The gate not opening under sustained KV pressure is the intended behavior, not a repeat of the earlier bug.
