SLO attainment, redefined: splitting the joint metric that collapsed to ~11%

The Llama-3.1-8B / 24GB sweep's slo_attainment averaged 0.112 across all 15 cells — not because the system was performing badly across the board, but because it's an AND of two SLOs with very different headroom at this load. Splitting it into ttft_attainment and tpot_attainment shows which constraint is actually failing.

Same 15 cells: reason × rates 2/4/8 × 5 arms TTFT SLO: 2.0s TPOT SLO: 0.040s specloop_rt/analysis.py

The original definition, unchanged in spirit, still lives in end_metrics():

slo_ok = ((done.ttft_s <= ttft_slo) & (done.tpot_s <= tpot_slo)).mean()

A request counts as attaining SLO only if both its time-to-first-token and its mean inter-token latency come in under threshold — an AND over two independent conditions, not an average. That's the right definition for "did this request feel fast," but it has a blind spot: if either SLO is structurally unreachable at a given load, the joint number goes to zero and tells you nothing about the other one.

Why the joint number is misleading here

TTFT p50 alone ranges 18s–252s against a 2.0s target — 9× to 126× over, purely from admission queueing at these rates on a 24GB card. TPOT stays much closer: p50 sits between 0.023s and 0.044s against a 0.040s target, i.e. right around the line. The AND definition means every request that waits too long for its first token fails the whole SLO regardless of how good its decode latency was — which is correct per-request semantics, but it makes the aggregate number read as "everything is broken" when really one specific mechanism (admission) is the bottleneck.

The fix isn't to loosen the threshold until the number looks better — that would just relabel the same queueing problem as "passing." The fix is to report the two constraints separately so a tight one doesn't silently erase what the other is telling you.

The new fields

specloop_rt/analysis.py, end_metrics() — two additions alongside the unchanged joint metric, computed from the same per-request ttft_s/tpot_s arrays already loaded.

ttft_ok = (done.ttft_s <= ttft_slo).mean()
tpot_ok = (done.tpot_s <= tpot_slo).mean()
...
"slo_attainment": float(slo_ok),        # unchanged: joint AND
"ttft_attainment": float(ttft_ok),      # new: TTFT alone
"tpot_attainment": float(tpot_ok),      # new: TPOT alone

All three attainment definitions, per arm and rate

Read

Both split metrics degrade with rate, as expected under more load — but at very different rates of decay. TTFT attainment falls from ~41% (rate=2) to ~7% (rate=8), a collapse driven by queueing depth scaling with arrival rate. TPOT attainment falls more gently, from ~54–57% (rate=2) to ~35–37% (rate=8) for the four higher-cap arms — decode latency degrades too under contention, just not as catastrophically. static-cap-lo is the outlier: its tight admission cap keeps concurrency low enough that TPOT attainment stays near 100% at every rate, at the direct cost of the worst TTFT attainment of any arm (its cap is the queueing bottleneck by construction).
