specloop · Axis-3 sweep report

The admission cap already does the work. Adding KV-awareness and a gated speculation loop didn't move it further.

Five controller arms, four workload types, four arrival rates — 80 cells on a real vLLM v0.9.2 engine, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, RTX A6000. The KV-aware ceiling and the gated speculation loop both did no harm, but neither had a regime to prove itself in on this hardware.

Static admission at a generous cap (max_num_seqs=256) is already close to as good as this controller family gets on this hardware. Adaptive-cap, KV-aware-cap, and the full gated controller all land within noise of the static baseline — sometimes a hair better, sometimes a hair worse, with no consistent winner by workload or rate. The one arm that is clearly worse is the tight static cap, confirming the floor is real. What actually decides the outcome, in every arm alike, is workload type and arrival rate — the same axis this sweep's two predecessors already located.

Why the KV ceiling never engaged

KVAwareAdmit's KV-headroom ceiling starts tightening admission once kv_used_frac crosses 0.80. Across all 80 cells, the worst KV occupancy measured was 0.365 — less than half the trigger point. A 7B model on a 48GB card at these rates never gets close to running out of KV blocks, so the ceiling term was mathematically a no-op the entire sweep. This isn't a bug in the controller; it's a sign the disturbance it was built to react to didn't occur on this hardware.
